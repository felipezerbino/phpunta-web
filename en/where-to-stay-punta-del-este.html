Punta del Este Guide

Where to stay in Punta del Este.

Every area has its own pace. Here is the coast, neighborhood by neighborhood, so you can pick the one that fits your trip.

Updated on November 17, 2026ES · PT · EN
In short

For a car-free trip with everything within walking distance, the Peninsula. With kids, Playa Mansa and its calm water. For waves and full-service buildings, Playa Brava. For restaurants and summer nightlife, La Barra; for beach and surf, Manantiales; for quiet and exclusivity, José Ignacio. For a house with a garden, San Rafael and Pinares; for countryside and space, the country estates of Laguna del Sauce.

Steps from the Peninsula

The Peninsula

The heart of Punta del Este: the port, the lighthouse and Gorlero Avenue.

The point that gives the city its name. Between Playa Mansa and Playa Brava, the Peninsula holds the port, the lighthouse, Gorlero Avenue with its shops and restaurants, and low-rise streets that still keep the feel of the original seaside town.

It is the area for those who want to leave the car parked: everything is within walking distance, the sea is one or two blocks away in every direction, and the sun sets over the port.

Steps from the Peninsula

Playa Mansa

Calm water, a long promenade and the sun setting over the sea.

Playa Mansa is the bay side, west of the Peninsula: calm water, wide sand and a promenade that follows the beach for miles. The first stops sit right next to the Peninsula; further west it becomes quieter and more residential.

It is the favorite of families with children and of anyone who wants to swim without waves. It is also the sunset side: the sun drops into the water with Gorriti Island in the background.

Playa Brava

Open ocean, La Mano and the buildings facing the sea.

The coast facing the Atlantic, on the east side. It starts at La Mano (the Hand), the most photographed monument in the country, and runs toward La Barra with waves, wide beaches and the Lorenzo Batlle Pacheco promenade.

The first stops are lined with buildings that offer pools, gyms and services, close to everything. Further along, toward San Rafael and Rincón del Indio, it turns more residential.

Steps from the Peninsula

Roosevelt and Aidy Grill

The practical center, halfway between Mansa and Brava.

The area built around Roosevelt Avenue: Aidy Grill, Cantegril and Punta Shopping, with supermarkets and services at hand and both beaches a few blocks away.

A comfortable base for longer stays and for anyone who wants everything nearby without the bustle of the Peninsula.

San Rafael

Houses among the pines, a few blocks from Brava.

A leafy residential neighborhood next to Playa Brava, with quiet streets and houses with gardens. It has the pace of a neighborhood, yet the ocean is a few blocks away and the Peninsula a few minutes by car.

A great choice for groups and families looking for a house with outdoor space and calm, without moving away from the center.

La Barra

The wavy bridge, galleries, restaurants and summer nights.

You arrive by crossing the wavy bridge over the Maldonado creek, one of the classic images of the coast. La Barra is the village with the most personality in the area: art galleries, design shops, restaurants and, in high season, the nightlife of Punta del Este.

Its beaches, such as Montoya, face the open ocean and are popular with surfers. Off season, it goes back to the pace of a quiet village.

Manantiales

Bikini Beach, El Chorro and sunsets over the dunes.

Past La Barra, along Route 10, Manantiales combines open-ocean beaches, dunes and houses with views. Bikini Beach is its summer meeting point, with a young crowd and long sunsets.

Outside high season it is a quiet seaside town, ideal for nature and open space a few minutes from La Barra.

José Ignacio

The lighthouse, the fishing village and the most sought-after calm on the coast.

A small fishing village turned into the most exclusive destination on the Uruguayan coast. The lighthouse, dirt roads, low houses and a dining scene known across the region give it a unique character.

It is for those seeking silence, nature and privacy, with lagoons nearby and beaches on both sides of the village. A car is needed.

Punta Ballena and Pinares

Casapueblo, Solanas and the hills that run into the sea.

West of the city, Punta Ballena is the ridge that runs into the sea and separates Maldonado Bay from Portezuelo Bay. It is home to Casapueblo, the work of artist Carlos Páez Vilaró, and some of the best sunsets on the coast.

Pinares, between Mansa and Punta Ballena, is a neighborhood of houses among the woods. The whole area is residential and best enjoyed with a car.

Laguna del Sauce

Country estates and farmhouses close to the airport.

The countryside of Punta del Este: country estates (chacras), houses with large grounds and the lagoon, a few minutes from the airport. The option for space, greenery and privacy, with the beach a short drive away.

Ideal for large groups, families and longer stays. A car is needed.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to stay on Playa Mansa or Playa Brava?

Mansa has calm water and sunsets over the sea: it is the favorite of families with children. Brava is open ocean, with waves, and has many buildings with pools and services. Both are a few minutes from the Peninsula.

Do I need a car in Punta del Este?

On the Peninsula, the first stops of Mansa and Brava and around Roosevelt you can do everything on foot. For La Barra, Manantiales, José Ignacio, Punta Ballena, Pinares or Laguna del Sauce a car is recommended.

Where is the nightlife in summer?

In high season, La Barra and Manantiales concentrate much of the nightlife, together with the Peninsula and the port.

What is the quietest area?

José Ignacio, Punta Ballena, Pinares, San Rafael and the country estates of Laguna del Sauce are the quietest, even in high season.
